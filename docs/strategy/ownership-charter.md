# Ashbi ownership charter

Status: Proposed for Bianca and Cameron approval

## Principle

Delivery may be shared, but every account, project, next action, approval, and escalation has one named owner. “Shared” is not a valid task owner.

## Default responsibilities

| Area | Cameron | Bianca |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead qualification and pipeline hygiene | Accountable | Consulted for creative-fit leads |
| Discovery and scope | Leads web, systems, technical, and commercial scope | Leads identity, packaging, production, and creative scope |
| Client account ownership | Named per account | Named per account |
| Creative direction | Consulted | Accountable |
| Illustrator and production artwork | Support as needed | Accountable |
| Web architecture and development | Accountable | Consulted on experience and visual direction |
| Custom applications, integrations, AI, and automation | Accountable | Consulted on workflow and client experience |
| Project planning and Hub record quality | Accountable for system | Accountable for assigned work accuracy |
| Proposal and contract approval | Accountable | Reviews creative scope and commitments |
| Invoice, payment, credential, production, and cutover approval | Accountable | Informed or consulted as applicable |
| Case-study display permission and contribution accuracy | Accountable for evidence record | Confirms creative contribution and assets |

These defaults do not assign public job titles or imply work history. Each project record may deliberately override them.

## Required ownership fields

Every qualified opportunity and active project must record:

- Account owner: Bianca or Cameron.
- Delivery lead: Bianca, Cameron, or Shared.
- Next-action owner: exactly one person.
- Approver: exactly one person for the current gate.
- Client decision-maker and contact method.
- Service line and capability state.
- Current stage, next action, due date, and blocker.
- Evidence/display-permission state when public use is contemplated.

## Approval matrix

Human approval is required before:

- Sending client or prospect communication.
- Publishing public content or client work.
- Issuing or accepting proposals and contracts.
- Creating live invoices, charges, refunds, subscriptions, or financial automations.
- Connecting accounts, changing credentials, billing, DNS, or production configuration.
- Deploying to production or changing a live client environment.
- Deleting, merging, or irreversibly transforming source records.
- Allowing an AI workflow to act on sensitive client data.

## Operating rhythm

### Daily

- Each person sees only owned next actions, approvals, due work, and blockers first.
- New inbox or lead items are assigned or explicitly parked; they do not remain ownerless.

### Weekly

- Review pipeline, active projects, overdue tasks, upcoming approvals, capacity, invoices, and receivables.
- Resolve duplicate or conflicting records before adding new parallel trackers.
- Choose the smallest complete launch or delivery slice for the following week.

### Monthly

- Review revenue by currency and service line without treating revenue as profit.
- Review estimate accuracy only where time and cost evidence is complete.
- Review recurring support capacity, failed automations, client issues, and evidence eligible for marketing.

### Quarterly

- Reapprove positioning, service states, pricing approach, target customer, and tool-retirement gates.

## Escalation rules

- Client scope, deadline, or commercial ambiguity goes to the account owner.
- Creative-quality or production-readiness ambiguity goes to Bianca.
- Architecture, security, integration, deployment, or data ambiguity goes to Cameron.
- Financial and production mutations stop until Cameron explicitly approves the exact action.
- If an automation cannot prove delivery, it records an unknown or reconciliation-required state and does not silently retry a representational or financial action.
